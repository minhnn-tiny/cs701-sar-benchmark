\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Multi-task learning.}
Hard parameter sharing trains one trunk for all tasks, and loss weighting~\cite{kendall2018multi}, gradient
normalisation~\cite{chen2018gradnorm} and gradient surgery~\cite{yu2020pcgrad} balance tasks whose losses differ in
scale and speed.
They assume the tasks compete.
Ours mostly cooperate, and the classification loss falls to near zero within 60 epochs, so we gained more by coupling
the heads at inference (\cref{sec:decoding}) than we could expect from re-weighting them.

\paragraph{Parameter-efficient adaptation.}
LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} and its multi-task forms, task-specific modules (MTLoRA~\cite{agiza2024mtlora}) and mixtures of
low-rank experts (MLoRE~\cite{yang2024mlore}), adapt a frozen encoder with about 1\,\% of its parameters; the course
reference applies them to ViT-B/16~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit} and TerraMind~\cite{jakubik2025terramind} with a ViTDet
pyramid~\cite{li2022vitdet}.
They are cheap and keep the pretrained features, but the features stay at stride 16 and frozen weights cap detection:
our two LoRA designs reached val $\Delta_m$ +7.1 at most (\cref{sec:journey}).

\paragraph{Object detectors.}
Two-stage detectors with feature pyramids (Faster R-CNN~\cite{ren2015faster}, FPN~\cite{lin2017fpn}, Cascade
R-CNN~\cite{cai2018cascade}) localise precisely but are heavy and slow to train.
DETR~\cite{carion2020detr} drops anchors and NMS but converges slowly and is weak on small objects;
RT-DETR~\cite{zhao2024rtdetr} makes it real-time.
YOLOv10~\cite{wang2024yolov10} pairs a one-to-many head with an NMS-free one-to-one head, and YOLO26~\cite{jocher2026yolo26}
keeps both, drops distribution focal loss, and adds progressive loss balancing and small-target-aware assignment.
Dense stride-8 heads suit tiny objects, and COCO pretraining~\cite{lin2014coco} brings necks and heads that an
image-level encoder lacks.

\paragraph{SAR detection and augmentation.}
The benchmarks behind our data~\cite{li2024sardet,zhang2025rsar,wang2025m4sar,wei2020hrsid} mostly fine-tune
detectors pretrained on natural images; SARDet-100K studies the domain gap this leaves.
SAR augmentation can follow the speckle model~\cite{argenti2013speckle}: an $L$-look intensity image is the
reflectivity times Gamma noise of mean 1 and variance $1/L$.
Generic augmentations such as mosaic~\cite{bochkovskiy2020yolov4} and copy-paste~\cite{ghiasi2021copypaste} add
context but change the scene layout.

\paragraph{Weight averaging.}
SWA~\cite{izmailov2018swa} averages weights along one training trajectory and finds flatter optima; model
soups~\cite{wortsman2022soups} average separately fine-tuned models, which works only when they share a basin.
